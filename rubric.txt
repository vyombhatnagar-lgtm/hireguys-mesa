PRODUCT MANAGER RUBRIC
==================================================================

1. Criterion name: Did the operational work firsthand
   What a strong candidate looks like: Has personally done the day-to-day
   work of an operations team (handled shipment documents, cleared customs
   holds, coordinated carriers, resolved delivery exceptions) as their own
   job, not as a consultant, interviewer or analyst watching others. A strong
   CV names specific documents, counterparties or volumes they handled
   (e.g. "200+ shipments monthly", "coordinated with CHA and customs
   overnight"). Weak: operations appear only as "user research",
   "discovery calls" or domain words in the summary.
   Weight: 25%
   Source: Rohan (CHA ops), Lavanya (Mahindra carrier ops), Meghna (freight docs).

2. Criterion name: Built something unprompted that others adopted
   What a strong candidate looks like: Spotted a problem in their own team,
   built a fix nobody assigned (a tool, tracker, checklist or process), and
   gives evidence that other people chose to use it: a number of users, teams
   that adopted it, or a usage change. Weak: counts of features shipped,
   documents written or talks given, with no evidence anyone used them.
   Weight: 25%
   Source: Rohan (BoL prototype, 30 users in a month), Lavanya (dashboard
   adopted by 2 regions), Meghna (checklist became team standard).

3. Criterion name: Killed or failed, and wrote down why
   What a strong candidate looks like: Names at least one thing they stopped,
   lost or got wrong, states what the evidence showed, and says what changed
   as a result (e.g. "killed 2 features after low adoption, moved the
   capacity to X, which got 3x usage"). Weak: every bullet is a win, or
   failure appears only as an abstract lesson.
   Weight: 20%
   Source: Lavanya (killed 2 features, outage post-mortem), Aditya (lost-deal
   post-mortem became standard practice).

4. Criterion name: No layer between them and the user
   What a strong candidate looks like: Worked directly with the people using
   the product or service and made the call themselves (sole owner of an
   area, no manager or product layer relaying requirements). A strong CV says
   who they dealt with directly. Weak: works through account managers, a
   larger PM team, or approval committees.
   Weight: 15%
   Source: Rohan (field requirements to spec with no product layer), Lavanya
   (sole PM), Aditya (no account-manager layer).

5. Criterion name: Fixed it personally under time pressure
   What a strong candidate looks like: Describes a specific breakdown (a
   customs hold, a vendor format change, an outage, a failed migration) that
   they personally resolved against a clock, with the outcome stated.
   Weak: pressure described only as "fast-paced environment".
   Weight: 15%
   Source: Meghna (overnight customs hold), Sunita (weekend workflow redesign
   after vendor change), Rohan (vendor migration with no data loss).

   PM TOTAL: 100%

==================================================================
SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER RUBRIC
(Same criteria, higher bar on independence.)
==================================================================

1. Criterion name: Did the operational work firsthand
   What a strong candidate looks like: Everything in the PM version, plus
   the operational experience directly shaped a product or system decision
   they later made (e.g. ops knowledge of port or customs systems led them to
   prioritise a specific integration). Weak: ops experience that sits
   unrelated to their product work.
   Weight: 20%
   Source: Rohan (CHA ops then freight platform engineering).

2. Criterion name: Built something unprompted that others adopted
   What a strong candidate looks like: Started something without being asked
   that became part of the core product or the organisation's standard way
   of working, used beyond their own team. Weak: adoption limited to their
   own immediate team, or built only when assigned.
   Weight: 15%
   Source: Rohan (BoL prototype became a core platform feature).

3. Criterion name: Killed or failed, and wrote down why
   What a strong candidate looks like: Made and owned a call with lasting
   consequences that turned out wrong or was reversed (a failed integration,
   a vendor choice, an architecture choice), and names the lasting change
   they made afterwards (a new standard, a process or a practice others now
   follow). Weak: only feature-level kills, or failures blamed on others.
   Weight: 20%
   Source: Aditya (post-mortem became standard qualification practice),
   Lavanya (post-mortem and follow-through to closure).

4. Criterion name: No layer between them and the user
   What a strong candidate looks like: Was the final decision-maker for a
   product area or function with no senior PM, VP or committee reviewing
   their calls, for a sustained period, and says so explicitly. Weak: the
   title says Senior or Head of, but the CV shows roadmaps reviewed by a VP,
   long approval cycles, or shared ownership.
   Weight: 30%
   Source: Rahul (no CMO above, reports to CEO), Lavanya (sole PM; decisions
   trusted immediately), Rohan (most senior engineer, no product layer).

5. Criterion name: Fixed it personally under time pressure
   What a strong candidate looks like: Led resolution of a breakdown that
   affected customers or revenue (a migration, a major outage, a
   data-quality failure) and changed the system so it did not recur (e.g.
   an on-call rotation that cut P1 incidents by 40%). Weak: a one-off fix
   with no systemic change.
   Weight: 15%
   Source: Rohan (vendor migration + on-call rotation, -40% P1 incidents).

   SPM TOTAL: 100%

==================================================================
SCORING GUIDE (both roles)
==================================================================
Score each criterion 0–5:
  5 = specific, dated evidence with a number or named outcome
  3 = evidence present but vague, or only partly the candidate's own
  1 = mentioned only in the summary, with no supporting bullet
  0 = absent
Weighted score = sum of (score / 5 × weight). Maximum 100.
Always recompute tenure from job dates. Several CVs' summaries are out of
date and do not match the dates.
